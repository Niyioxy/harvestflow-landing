About HarvestFlow

HarvestFlow helps churches make sure that every person who responds at an altar call, or walks through the door for the first time, is followed up by name, and that no one quietly slips away.

Why HarvestFlow exists

Churches are often good at reaching people. The outreach is full of energy and the altar call is answered. The harder question comes afterwards: who is responsible for these people now?

Too often the answer is a pile of cards in an office, a spreadsheet only one person can open, or a WhatsApp group where everyone assumes someone else has called. People who made a real decision, or took a brave first step into a church, hear nothing and drift away.

HarvestFlow was built to close that gap. Every person is registered on the day, given to one named worker, called within days, and followed from their first contact into the life of the church. Pastors can see who has been reached and who has not, without chasing anyone for updates. As Paul said to Barnabas, "Let us go again and visit our brethren … and see how they do" (Acts 15:36).

Who builds it

Adeniyi Owolabi, founder

HarvestFlow is built and maintained by Adeniyi Owolabi, a software developer and church leader based in the United Kingdom. He started HarvestFlow after seeing how easily new converts and visitors were lost between the altar and the pew, and wanted to give churches a simple tool their workers would actually use on a busy week.

He also builds Songflow, a free worship lyrics app for churches.

What we believe

Every person has a name

Follow-up is pastoral care, not data entry. The software exists so that a real person calls, prays and invites.

Your church's data is yours

Each church has its own private workspace, every role sees only what it should, and you can download your records at any time. Read our privacy policy.

Built with churches

HarvestFlow is in beta, shaped by the churches using it every week, starting with Perfection Bible Church, our first partner church.

For churches worldwide

It runs in any phone or computer browser, works offline for field workers, and reads phone numbers in each church's own country.
